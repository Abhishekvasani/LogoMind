# LM-STD-003 — Confidence Framework

> Confidence measures **how strongly supported** a knowledge statement is — not how certain we feel.

---

## 1. Purpose

Not every piece of design knowledge has the same strength. "A circle suggests unity" is near-universal; "purple suggests luxury" depends heavily on context. LogoMind must distinguish these honestly, so designers trust the reasoning.

---

## 2. The Five Confidence Levels

| Level | Label | Emoji | Meaning |
|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| **C5** | Foundational | 🟢 | Strongly supported across cultures, industries, and time. Rarely contested. |
| **C4** | Strong | 🔵 | Generally reliable; broad professional agreement. |
| **C3** | Moderate | 🟠 | Context-dependent; works in many cases but not all. |
| **C2** | Emerging | 🟣 | Early or limited support; useful but unproven at scale. |
| **C1** | Exploratory | ⚪ | Hypothesis or creative conjecture; presented as such. |

---

## 3. Assignment Criteria

Confidence is assigned based on six factors:

1. **Cross-cultural consistency** — Does this hold across regions?
2. **Historical durability** — Has it held over decades?
3. **Empirical support** — Is there documented evidence?
4. **Professional consensus** — Do experienced practitioners agree?
5. **Reproducibility** — Does it produce consistent outcomes?
6. **Industry breadth** — Does it apply across industries or only some?

---

## 4. Relationship to Statement Taxonomy (LM-STD-002)

| Statement Type | Typical Confidence |
|----------------|--------------------|
| Definition | C5 (Foundational) |
| Principle | C4–C5 |
| Framework | C3–C4 |
| Method | C3–C4 |
| Guideline | C2–C3 |
| Observation | C2–C3 |
| Interpretation | C2–C3 |
| Open Question | C1 (Exploratory) |

---

## 5. Editorial Rules

1. **Every significant claim carries a confidence label.**
2. **Confidence must be justified** — a Confidence Justification Note explains *why* the level was assigned.
3. **Confidence drift is tracked** — if a claim's support weakens or strengthens over time, the label is reviewed.
4. **Confidence is not agreement** — a statement can be C5 (strongly supported) and still contested by some practitioners. Honesty about support level is separate from universal acceptance.
5. **Low confidence is not failure** — marking something C1 or C2 is intellectual honesty, not a weakness.

---

## 6. Example

> **"Circles are associated with unity and wholeness."**
> Confidence: 🟢 C5 (Foundational)
> Justification: Cross-cultural, historically durable ( millennia), broad consensus, reproducible across industries.

> **"Purple communicates luxury."**
> Confidence: 🟠 C3 (Moderate)
> Justification: Context-dependent — varies by industry, region, and cultural association. Strong in Western luxury markets; weaker elsewhere.

## Relationship to Other Standards

- **LM-STD-002** (Statement Taxonomy) defines *what kind* of statement it is; this standard defines *how strongly* it is supported.
- **LM-STD-005** (Knowledge Layering) determines *where* claims of each confidence level appear.
- **LM-STD-006** (Quality Review) verifies confidence labels are present and justified.

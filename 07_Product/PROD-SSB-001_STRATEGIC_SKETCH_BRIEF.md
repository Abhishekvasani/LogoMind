# PROD-SSB-001 — Strategic Sketch Brief (SSB) Template

> *The SSB is LogoMind's flagship output. It is not a logo. It is not a concept. It is the complete creative strategy the designer needs to sketch with confidence — the strongest strategic foundation they've ever had before putting pen to paper.*

---

## 1. Purpose

The Strategic Sketch Brief (SSB) is the **primary deliverable** of the LOGOS pipeline (FD-014). It is what the designer receives after Discovery, Strategy, Insight, Create, and Judge have done their work.

The SSB does not tell the designer what to draw. It tells them:
- What the design must accomplish strategically
- Which creative directions are worth exploring (and why)
- What to avoid (and why)
- How the Creative Council would advise them
- Specific sketch missions to start from

The designer still sketches. The designer still decides. The SSB gives them the strongest possible foundation.

---

## 2. The 5-Minute Rule (Product Law)

> *An SSB must be readable in 5 minutes.*

If the SSB takes longer than 5 minutes to absorb, it has failed. The designer needs to internalise the strategy quickly and get to sketching. Depth is available on demand (Progressive Disclosure), but the surface must be fast.

---

## 3. The Seven Sections

Every SSB has exactly seven sections, in fixed order:

| # | Section | Purpose | Length |
|---|---------|---------|--------|
| 1 | **Project Essence** | One-paragraph synthesis — what is this project? | 3–5 sentences |
| 2 | **Brand DNA Snapshot** | The six strands, condensed | 1 page |
| 3 | **Creative North Star** | The single sentence the logo must embody | 1 sentence |
| 4 | **Creative Territories** | The chosen family + 1–2 alternatives | 2–3 pages |
| 5 | **Opportunities & Warnings** | What to explore; what to avoid | 1 page |
| 6 | **Creative Council Advice** | The 9 minds' guidance, in a table | 1 page |
| 7 | **Sketch Missions** | 5–10 specific starting points with rationale | 2–3 pages |

Total: 8–13 pages, but scannable in 5 minutes at the surface level.

---

## 4. Section 1 — Project Essence

### Purpose
Give the designer the strategic gist in one paragraph.

### Template
```
PROJECT ESSENCE

[Company Name] is a [category] serving [audience configuration in
brief]. Their strategic foundation is [positioning summary]. The
identity must express [core meaning] while differentiating from
[primary competitors] through [key differentiator]. The brand's
character is [personality in brief], and the audience should feel
[emotional goal] upon encountering the identity.
```

### Example
```
PROJECT ESSENCE

Northbridge Coffee is an independent specialty roaster serving urban
professionals seeking calm moments during busy workdays. Their
strategic foundation is craft + community — they position as the
neighbourhood's "third place" for considered pause. The identity
must express warmth and craftsmanship while differentiating from
chain coffee through hand-made, human signals rather than corporate
polish. The brand's character is the calm, attentive host, and the
audience should feel welcomed and slowed-down upon encountering the
identity.
```

## 6. Section 3 — Creative North Star

### Purpose
The single sentence the logo must embody — the criterion against which every design decision is evaluated.

### Template
```
CREATIVE NORTH STAR

The [Company Name] logo must make [audience] feel [emotional goal]
by expressing [core meaning] through [visual character] that
differentiates from [competitors] through [key differentiator].

ONE-SENTENCE TEST
Before finalising any concept, ask:
"Does this make [audience] feel [emotion]?"
If not, the concept has failed the North Star — regardless of
its aesthetic quality.
```

### Example
```
CREATIVE NORTH STAR

The Northbridge Coffee logo must make busy professionals feel
welcomed and slowed-down by expressing craft + community through
warm, hand-made character that differentiates from chain coffee
through human (not corporate) signals.
```

## 10. Section 7 — Sketch Missions

### Purpose
Give the designer 5–10 specific starting points — each a clear, actionable sketch direction with rationale.

### Template
```
SKETCH MISSIONS

Each mission is a starting point, not a prescription. Pick the ones
that resonate; ignore the rest. Sketch broadly before narrowing.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MISSION 1: <mission name>

CORE IDEA:
<one-sentence description of what to explore>

COMBINE:
· <element 1>
· <element 2>
· <element 3 (optional)>

WHY IT WORKS:
<2-3 sentences tracing to Brand DNA + Creative Council>

POTENTIAL PITFALLS:
· <pitfall 1>
· <pitfall 2>

START WITH:
<specific guidance — "begin with a circular grid", "explore
negative space forming the letter N", "test at favicon size early">

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MISSION 2: <mission name>
[same structure]

[Continue for Missions 3–10]
```

### Example Mission
```
MISSION 3: Pour Geometry

CORE IDEA:
Explore the geometry of the coffee pour as a craft signal.

COMBINE:
· Pour curve (the arc of coffee from vessel to cup)
· Letter N (subtle integration, not forced)
· Negative space (what the pour curve encloses)

WHY IT WORKS:
The pour is the moment of craft — the barista's signature act.
Encoding it in the mark signals craft without using the exhausted
coffee-bean or steam-swirl clichés. The negative space offers a
secondary meaning opportunity (the enclosed form could suggest
community — being held).

POTENTIAL PITFALLS:
· Pour curves can read as "swoosh" if too generic — make it specific
· Letter N integration risks forced monogram — let it emerge from
  the geometry rather than imposing it

START WITH:
Sketch 10 pour curves from observation (visit the café, watch the
baristas). Don't stylise yet — just observe the geometry. Then
explore which curves naturally suggest a letterform in their
negative space. Test at favicon size from the beginning.
```

## 11. Progressive Disclosure Layers

The SSB has three reading layers (per LM-STD-005):

### Layer A — Essence (5 minutes)
- Section 1 (Project Essence)
- Section 3 (Creative North Star)
- Section 7 (Sketch Missions — skim titles)

*The designer gets the strategic gist and starting points.*

### Layer B — Professional (15 minutes)
- All seven sections at surface level
- Brand DNA Snapshot (not full Brand DNA)
- Creative Territories (recommended family in detail)

*The designer has everything needed to begin sketching with intention.*

### Layer C — Deep (on demand)
- Full Brand DNA (link from Section 2)
- Full Judge Report (link from Section 4)
- Full Insight Report (link from Section 5)
- Reasoning trails for every recommendation

*Available when the designer wants to understand WHY, not just WHAT.*

## 13. Quality Checks

| Check | Question | Pass Criterion |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| **5-Minute Rule** | Can the SSB be absorbed in 5 minutes (Layer A)? | Yes — essence + North Star + skim missions |
| **North Star Clarity** | Is the single sentence unambiguous? | Yes — actionable test for every decision |
| **Sketch Missions Actionable** | Can the designer start sketching from each mission? | Yes — specific starting points, not vague themes |
| **Reasoning Traceable** | Can every recommendation be traced to Brand DNA + Council + Judge? | Yes — reasoning links available |
| **Cliché Avoidance Visible** | Are clichés explicitly flagged? | Yes — Section 5 |
| **Client Respectful Challenge** | Are weak client requests surfaced with alternatives? | Yes — Creative Director Flag |

---

## 14. Failure Cases

| Failure | What Happens | Prevention |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| **Too Long** | SSB exceeds 5-minute absorb time | 5-Minute Rule; surface must be scannable |
| **Vague Missions** | Sketch Missions are themes, not starting points | Missions must include specific "start with" guidance |
| **Unsupported Recommendations** | Recommendations without reasoning trails | Reasoning Traceable check; every claim sourced |
| **Cliché Compliance** | SSB includes overused symbols without flag | Cliché Avoidance check; consult Insight Report |
| **Decision Lock-In** | SSB prescribes rather than guides | Missions are starting points, not requirements |
| **Hidden Uncertainty** | SSB presents low-confidence recommendations as certain | Confidence levels visible per LM-STD-003 |

## 15. The SSB and the Product Promise

The SSB embodies LogoMind's core promise:

> *LogoMind will never make a creative decision for the designer.*

The SSB:
- Provides direction; the designer chooses direction
- Provides sketch missions; the designer sketches
- Provides evaluation criteria; the designer evaluates
- Provides reasoning; the designer defends

It does NOT:
- Provide a finished logo
- Tell the designer what to draw
- Replace the designer's craft
- Make the creative decision

The SSB is the strongest possible foundation that still leaves the designer sovereign.

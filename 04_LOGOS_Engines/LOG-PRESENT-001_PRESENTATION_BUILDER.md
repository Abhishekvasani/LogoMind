# LOG-PRESENT-001 — Presentation Builder

> *Clients buy the reasoning as much as the design. The Presentation Builder assembles a client-ready explanation from the project's full history — strategy, exploration, decision, execution — so the client understands not just what the logo is, but why it is.*

---

## 1. Mission

Transform the complete project history (Discovery → Brand DNA → Concept Families → sketches → final concept) into a coherent, client-ready presentation that explains the logo's *reasoning* — not just its appearance. The presentation must convince the client that the chosen concept is strategically sound, not merely aesthetically pleasing.

---

## 2. Purpose in the Pipeline

```
[Full project history] →  LOGOS Present  →  Client Presentation
(Discovery, Strategy,       (assembles the      (the client
 Insight, Create, Judge,     narrative)          buys the reasoning)
 Coach, final concept)
                                ▲
                                │
                       THE PRESENTATION BUILDER
                       tells the strategic story
                       behind the design
```

The Presentation Builder is the final engine in the pipeline — it activates once a concept is finalised and the project is ready for client delivery.

---

## 3. The Core Principle: Reasoning Sells

> *A client who understands why the logo works becomes its defender. A client who only sees the logo becomes its critic.*

The Presentation Builder's job is to produce defenders, not critics. It does this by making the reasoning transparent — so the client can advocate for the design internally, against the inevitable "I don't like it" pushback from colleagues, bosses, and spouses.

---

## 4. Inputs

### Required Data
- The finalised concept (from Sketch Coach / designer's final choice)
- Brand DNA (the strategic foundation)
- Chosen Concept Family (and why it was chosen)
- Judge Report (the evaluation that supported the choice)
- Sketch history (key iterations and why they were refined)

### Optional Data
- Insight Report (competitive context for the presentation)
- Designer's reflection notes
- Client-specific presentation preferences

### Knowledge Sources
- **RS-LIC-PH-001 Meaning** — the concept's meaning is the presentation's spine
- **RS-LIC-BS-001 Brand Positioning** — how the concept serves the positioning
- **All relevant Philosophy LICs** — the presentation can explain each principle the concept honours

---

## 5. Reasoning Steps

```
Step 1: ESTABLISH THE NARRATIVE ARC
   → Every presentation tells a story with a structure:
     ├── Beginning: The brand's strategic foundation (purpose, positioning)
     ├── Middle: The exploration (Concept Families considered, why)
     ├── Climax: The chosen concept (and why it survived)
     ├── Resolution: How it works (construction, meaning, application)
     └── Future: How it will live (applications, system, guidelines)
   → The Presentation Builder assembles the project history into this arc

Step 2: CRAFT THE CONCEPT STORY
   → Tell the story of the chosen concept:
     ├── What is the core idea? (one-sentence essence)
     ├── What meaning does it carry? (RS-LIC-PH-001)
     ├── What strategic foundation does it serve? (Brand DNA)
     ├── What are the key design decisions? (form, colour, typography)
     └── Why does each decision earn its place?

Step 3: DEVELOP SYMBOL EXPLANATION
   → Explain the symbol's meaning:
     ├── Primary reading (the immediate meaning)
     ├── Secondary readings (layers, if present)
     ├── Cultural/historical context (if relevant)
     └── Connection to brand meaning (not arbitrary)

Step 4: ARTICULATE COLOUR RATIONALE
   → Explain the colour choice:
     ├── Emotional associations (primary, secondary, accent)
     ├── Industry context (differentiation from competitors)
     ├── Cultural considerations (if relevant)
     ├── Accessibility considerations (contrast, reproduction)
     └── Production considerations (PMS, CMYK, RGB equivalents)

Step 5: ARTICULATE TYPOGRAPHY RATIONALE
   → Explain the typography choice:
     ├── Personality match (how the typeface expresses the brand character)
     ├── Industry context (differentiation, convention)
     ├── Legibility considerations (sizes, applications)
     ├── Pairing logic (if multiple typefaces)
     └── Justification — why this typeface and not alternatives

Step 6: SHOW CONSTRUCTION LOGIC
   → Demonstrate the construction:
     ├── Grid/geometric system used
     ├── Proportions (intentional, not arbitrary)
     ├── Optical corrections (if any)
     └── "Why it looks right" — the underlying structure

Step 7: ADDRESS SCALABILITY & APPLICATIONS
   → Show the concept across applications:
     ├── Favicon (16px) — does it survive?
     ├── Business card — does it work at small scale?
     ├── Signage — does it scale up?
     ├── Monochrome — does it work without colour?
     ├── Reverse — does it work on dark backgrounds?
     └── Motion (if applicable) — does it animate well?

Step 8: EXPLAIN FUTURE-PROOFING
   → Address longevity explicitly:
     ├── Why this concept will age well (RS-LIC-PH-008)
     ├── Trend awareness (what was considered and rejected)
     ├── Flexibility (how it adapts to future touchpoints)
     └── Maintenance (how to keep the identity coherent)

Step 9: PREPARE FOR OBJECTIONS
   → Anticipate likely client concerns:
     ├── "It's too simple" → have the Reduction Sequence explanation ready
     ├── "Can we add [X]?" → have the simplicity defence ready
     ├── "I prefer [competitor]'s logo" → have the differentiation case ready
     ├── "My spouse doesn't like it" → have the strategic case ready
     └── "Can we see more options?" → have the Concept Family exploration ready
   → The Presentation Builder generates objection-handling appendices

Step 10: ASSEMBLE THE PRESENTATION
   → Compile all elements into the final presentation structure:
     1. Cover
     2. Executive summary (one page)
     3. Brand foundation (purpose, positioning)
     4. Strategic exploration (Concept Families considered)
     5. The chosen concept (story + visual)
     6. Design rationale (symbol, colour, typography, construction)
     7. Applications (scalability demonstrations)
     8. Future-proofing
     9. Brand guidelines summary
     10. Q&A preparation (objection-handling notes)
```

## 6. Decision Rules

| Rule | Condition | Action |
|------|-----------|--------|
| **DR-1** | Client cannot articulate their brand strategy | Begin presentation with strategy education, not concept reveal |
| **DR-2** | Concept carries hidden meaning that may be missed | Include explicit "discovery moment" explanation |
| **DR-3** | Client has history of rejecting work | Include extra objection-handling preparation |
| **DR-4** | Presentation audience includes non-design stakeholders | Translate design vocabulary into business vocabulary |
| **DR-5** | Concept is bold or unconventional | Spend more time on strategic justification; less on aesthetics |

---

## 7. Confidence Calculation

| Presentation Element | Confidence Basis |
|---------------------|------------------|
| Strategic case | Brand DNA + Positioning strength |
| Symbol meaning | Established meaning vs. interpretation |
| Colour rationale | Industry consensus + cultural verification |
| Longevity claim | Honest uncertainty acknowledged (per RS-LIC-PH-008) |
| Objection preparation | Anticipated based on concept type and client history |

The Presentation Builder is honest about uncertainty. Longevity claims acknowledge they can only be verified in retrospect (per RS-LIC-PH-008). Symbol meanings distinguish established from interpretive.

## 8. Outputs

### Primary Output: Client Presentation

Structure (10 sections — see Step 10 above), delivered as:
- Slide deck (Keynote, PowerPoint, PDF)
- Accompanying notes (for the designer's verbal presentation)
- Brand guidelines summary document (PDF)
- Q&A preparation notes (for the designer)

### Secondary Outputs
- **One-Page Summary** — for stakeholders who won't read the full deck
- **Brand Guidelines Skeleton** — the start of a full guidelines document
- **Internal Handoff Document** — for the team that will implement the identity

## 9. The Tone Principle

The Presentation Builder adopts three voices (per the Three Voices of LogoMind):

| Voice | When Used | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **The Mentor** (explains) | Educating the client on strategy | "Your positioning requires sacrifice — here's what we chose and why" |
| **The Strategist** (challenges) | Addressing concerns | "Adding [X] would dilute the meaning — here's the case against" |
| **The Creative Director** (applies) | Presenting the design | "This form carries [meaning] because [reasoning]" |

All three voices are present in a strong presentation — education, challenge, and craft together.

## 10. Quality Checks

| Check | Question | Pass Criterion |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| **Reasoning Transparency** | Can the client explain *why* the logo works, not just *what* it is? | Yes — the presentation teaches the reasoning |
| **Objection Preparation** | Are likely client concerns anticipated and addressed? | Yes — objection-handling appendix |
| **Honest Uncertainty** | Are longevity/meaning claims appropriately hedged? | Yes — no false certainty |
| **Strategic Foundation** | Does the presentation start with strategy, not aesthetics? | Yes — concept reveal comes after strategic case |
| **Application Coverage** | Does the presentation show the concept across applications? | Yes — favicon, signage, monochrome, reverse |
| **Business Vocabulary** | Is design vocabulary translated for non-design stakeholders? | Yes — accessibility for the audience |

---

## 11. Failure Cases

| Failure | What Happens | Prevention |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| **Aesthetics-First** | Presentation leads with the design, not the strategy | Strategic Foundation check; strategy first |
| **Unexplained Decisions** | Design choices presented without reasoning | Reasoning Transparency check; every choice justified |
| **False Certainty** | Longevity or meaning claims over-stated | Honest Uncertainty check; acknowledge limits |
| **Missing Applications** | Concept shown only in one context | Application Coverage check; show across contexts |
| **No Objection Prep** | Designer ambushed by predictable concerns | Objection Preparation check; anticipate and prepare |
| **Jargon Lock-In** | Presentation uses vocabulary the client doesn't share | Business Vocabulary check; translate design terms |

## The Reasoning Sells Principle

The Presentation Builder embodies one of LogoMind's deepest convictions:

> *Clients buy the reasoning as much as the design.*

A logo presented without reasoning is an aesthetic object vulnerable to aesthetic objections ("I don't like it"). A logo presented with reasoning is a strategic decision vulnerable only to strategic objections — which are easier to address because they can be answered with evidence.

The Presentation Builder's entire purpose is to move the conversation from aesthetics (where the client is expert in their own taste) to strategy (where the designer is expert in the brand's needs). This is the designer's strongest position — and the Presentation Builder exists to establish it.

# Client Psychology — Starter Set (8 Types + 2 Systems)

> *The hardest design problem in the room is rarely the mark — it is the person approving it. Read the decision-maker as carefully as the brief.*

Three systems: **decision-maker types** (recognise who approves, predict what they can love), the **feedback decoder** (translate taste-language into designable meaning), and the **objection taxonomy** (answer the question underneath the pushback).

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-001 — The Founder-Owner

**Recognition Cues:** First-person voice everywhere; the brand is their name or their story; "I want" outweighs "our audience needs"
**What They Value:** Personal pride and legacy — the mark is their flag; distinctiveness that reflects their conviction
**Aesthetic Lean:** Varies with personality; runs personal rather than category-conventional
**Boldness Tolerance:** Moderate to adventurous — their name is on it, so it must be nobody else's
**Feedback Style:** Fast, visceral, decisive — but swings hard between rounds
**How to Win Them:** Make them feel seen; show the mark wearing their conviction; present one direction you believe in, not a menu
**Watch Out:** Takes design disagreement personally — separate the brand's meaning from their autobiography
**LKG Relationships:** → Authenticity, → Identity

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-002 — The Committee

**Recognition Cues:** "Our team", "stakeholders", "board approval"; feedback arrives in batches from several voices
**What They Value:** Consensus and survival — everyone must leave the meeting comfortable
**Aesthetic Lean:** Regresses to neutral; boldness gets averaged out across reviewers
**Boldness Tolerance:** Conservative — the adventurous option dies in the second round
**Feedback Style:** Contradictory, cumulative, increasingly late
**How to Win Them:** Give the group a shared criteria language (the jury dimensions) so they debate evidence, not each other's taste
**Watch Out:** Design-by-averaging produces the gray mark everyone tolerates and nobody loves — surface the trade-offs instead of blending them
**LKG Relationships:** → Clarity, → Consistency

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-003 — The Marketing Director

**Recognition Cues:** Fluent brand jargon ("positioning", "equity", "tone of voice"); competitor references; trend awareness
**What They Value:** Differentiation, brand-system thinking, choices they can defend upward
**Aesthetic Lean:** Contemporary; system-aware
**Boldness Tolerance:** Moderate — bold if the category allows a story for it
**Feedback Style:** Articulate and criteria-based — but portfolio-biased (wants what worked at their last company)
**How to Win Them:** Speak strategy; trace every visual choice back to positioning; give them the deck THEY can present upward
**Watch Out:** Trend-chasing and borrowed distinctiveness — the strategy must be theirs, not a transplant of someone else's win
**LKG Relationships:** → Differentiation, → Relevance

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-004 — The Small-Business Pragmatist

**Recognition Cues:** Short practical brief; money/speed language; "something clean and simple"; the classic contest holder
**What They Value:** Value and utility — works everywhere, arrives fast, costs reason
**Aesthetic Lean:** Minimal; unfussy
**Boldness Tolerance:** Conservative — unusual is risk, and risk costs money
**Feedback Style:** Sparse and concrete: "bigger", "blue not green", "can the icon be the shop front"
**How to Win Them:** Show it working — on the sign, the card, the social avatar — rather than theorising; concrete beats abstract every round
**Watch Out:** Cannot articulate taste, so asks for decoration when they mean confidence; decode before adding things
**LKG Relationships:** → Clarity, → Simplicity

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-005 — The Heritage Guardian

**Recognition Cues:** Founded/year language; "our reputation"; references the previous mark or founder era; institutional categories
**What They Value:** Continuity and accumulated trust — the mark is a promise already kept for decades
**Aesthetic Lean:** Classic, refined
**Boldness Tolerance:** Conservative — novelty reads as disrespect
**Feedback Style:** Anchored to the past: "our old logo had...", "the founder chose..."
**How to Win Them:** Evolution, never revolution — show the lineage preserved in the new mark; every change justified as stewardship
**Watch Out:** Fear of change freezes the brand into datedness — the case to make is that heritage is served by remaining legible
**LKG Relationships:** → Timelessness, → Authenticity

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-006 — The Disruptor

**Recognition Cues:** "Bold", "different", "break the rules"; category anger ("the X industry is broken")
**What They Value:** Standing out; being remembered; press-worthiness
**Aesthetic Lean:** Bold; high-contrast; unconstrained
**Boldness Tolerance:** Adventurous — "safe" is an insult and they will escalate
**Feedback Style:** Pushes further each round; allergic to anything that resembles competitors
**How to Win Them:** Audacity WITH meaning — boldness traced to strategy beats boldness for its own sake; show them the mark nobody can un-see AND what it means
**Watch Out:** Shock without meaning — unrecognizable, off-category, off-putting; the audience still needs to trust the brand enough to buy
**LKG Relationships:** → Originality, → Differentiation

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-007 — The Technical Buyer

**Recognition Cues:** Feature and precision language; "scalable", "works at 16px"; asks for SVGs, print specs, clear-space rules
**What They Value:** Precision, consistency, engineering-grade execution
**Aesthetic Lean:** Technical, minimal, systematic
**Boldness Tolerance:** Moderate to conservative — decoration without function annoys them
**Feedback Style:** Specific and measurable: "stroke too thin for embroidery", "fails the favicon test"
**How to Win Them:** Show the system — grids, optical corrections, clear space, the scale test; craft they can verify
**Watch Out:** Over-engineering the mark — measurable virtues displacing meaning; a logo is a signal, not a spec sheet
**LKG Relationships:** → Clarity, → Consistency

---

## RS-LIC-PSY-008 — The Relayer

**Recognition Cues:** "I'll have to check with..."; feedback arrives as reported speech; the real decision-maker never appears
**What They Value:** A smooth relay and not being blamed; keeping both sides happy
**Aesthetic Lean:** Unknowable — the relay loses signal with every hop
**Boldness Tolerance:** Reads conservative by default (secondhand caution)
**Feedback Style:** Distorted, averaged, delayed: "they liked it, but..."
**How to Win Them:** Equip the relayer to succeed in the room you're not in — the rationale deck, the criteria, the answers to predictable objections; request the real audience where possible
**Watch Out:** Every relay flattens the design; you end up presenting to a distortion of the approver
**LKG Relationships:** → Clarity, → Communication

## RS-LIC-PSY-009 — The Feedback Decoder

Taste-language is compressed meaning. Decode the phrase, confirm the decoded meaning in the client's own words, then design the fix.

| They say | It usually means | The professional move |
|---|---|---|
| "Make it pop" | Nothing is leading — hierarchy is flat | Create one dominant contrast of weight, scale, or colour; add nothing |
| "Make it modern" | The treatment feels dated, usually the type | Refresh letterforms and spacing; do NOT transplant a trend |
| "Something clean" | Fear of clutter | Protect negative space; remove before adding |
| "Make it friendly" | Too cold or institutional | Warm the geometry (rounding, humanist type); not cartoons |
| "More premium" | Too busy or too plain | Restraint and contrast — spacing, not ornament |
| "I'll know it when I see it" | No criteria yet | Supply criteria via meaning and audience before more options |
| "Can it be bigger?" | The mark lacks presence | Add weight or simplify so it holds; literal size is rarely the issue |
| "Something like Nike/Apple" | They want the CONFIDENCE of those marks | Deliver decisiveness and simplicity — never the style |
| "Make it fun" | Feels inaccessible or stiff | Approachability through warmth and rhythm; not decoration |
| "Too simple" | Reads as unfinished | Show the craft (reduction, scale behaviour) — or add one meaningful detail |
| "My spouse hates it" | Single-anecdote override | Widen the evidence politely: audience, meaning, tests |
| "We want it to stand out" | Differentiation anxiety | Check the cliché list for the category; distinct within trust bounds |

## RS-LIC-PSY-010 — The Objection Taxonomy

Every objection is a question wearing armour. Answer the underlying question with craft evidence — never defend taste with taste.

| Objection | The question underneath | The response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "It's too simple" | Did real work go into this? | Walk the Reduction Sequence; show scale tests and favicon behaviour — simplicity as engineering |
| "It's too bold / risky" | Will this embarrass us? | Show the system flexing across contexts; show where restraint lives (colour, type discipline) |
| "I've seen something like it" | Are we derivative? | Run the originality tests; show the combination's specific lineage and what was consciously avoided |
| "Our competitors could use this" | Where is OUR mark? | Trace the mark to Brand DNA — the differentiator should be structurally visible |
| "It doesn't say what we do" | How will people know us? | A logo signals identity, not services; recognition is built by repetition, not description |
| "Why is typography so expensive?" | What am I paying for? | Letterform craft story — optical corrections, spacing, the wordmark as the most-seen asset |
| "Can we see more options?" | Are we sure this is THE one? | Re-present the chosen territory's reasoning; more options dilute, they don't clarify |
| "My kid could draw that" | Is this professional? | Professional simplicity (PH-003): the tests a child's drawing fails |
| "Can you just add..." | (Scope anxiety or a hidden concern) | Test every addition against the system; additions must earn their place like any element |

## Client Psychology Framework

| Situation | Rule |
|-------------------|------|
| Brief reveals a decision-maker type | Use the type's value/risk profile to PREDICT taste — but the brief's own evidence always overrides; types are priors, not verdicts |
| Vague or taste-language feedback | Decode with the glossary; confirm the decoded meaning in the client's words BEFORE redesigning |
| An objection arrives | It is a question wearing armour — answer the underlying question with craft evidence (tests, scale, system), never defend taste with taste |
| Stakeholders conflict | Design for the decision-maker, validate against the audience (RS-LIC-BS-003); surface the conflict — never silently average it |
| Predicting appeal | Appeal = type's values × the brief's specific signals; where they conflict, trust the brief |

---

*LogoMind Principle: The client's words are data. Taste-language is compressed meaning, objections are armoured questions, and every decision-maker type can be moved by evidence in their own dialect. The professional translates — and never mistakes a person for a stereotype.*
